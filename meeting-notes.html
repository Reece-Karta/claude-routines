US Tax and Relocation Planning with Bradley Murphy
10 Sep 2026, 10:06 AM GMT+10
Reece Munro Bradley Murphy

Summary

  • Reece and Courtney will cease being Australian tax residents upon leaving for the US, given the E-3 visa, permanent employment, and planned 6-year stay, with no Australian property or home base remaining
  • Non-residency is self-assessed and reported on the FY27 tax return (filed July 2027) rather than notified in advance
  • Remaining a non-resident is roughly 20% better off financially than staying an Australian tax resident, given Texas has no state income tax and Australian rates reach 47%
  • Australian investment income earned while overseas is reported to the ATO at a 10% withholding rate, handled by the bank once notified of non-residency
  • Superannuation (Australian Retirement Trust) can sit untouched while overseas; only a minimum contribution is needed to keep the fund active without fees, and no further super contributions are tax-effective while in the US
  • On return to Australia, a 5-year catch-up of unused concessional super contributions is available (up to ~$160,000)
  • Shares bought while a non-Australian, non-US resident are effectively free of Australian CGT if held until return to Australia; PFIC rules mean non-US ETFs/mutual funds should be avoided while in the US, but individual shares and US-domiciled funds are fine
  • The E-3 visa carries no green card path and no exit tax risk; FBAR reporting of all Australian bank accounts and super funds to the IRS is required annually
